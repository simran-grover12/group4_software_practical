# group4_software_practical
## Introduction

University campuses rely on multiple dining facilities, such as cafeterias, food outlets, and dining halls, to meet the daily food requirements of students, faculty, staff, and visitors.

## Related Work

Food ordering applications allow users to browse menus, place orders, and make payments for individual eateries, while university dining halls typically publish daily meal schedules and nutritional information through separate channels.

## Problem Statement

Because these services operate independently, users must switch between different sources of information and physical locations to decide what to eat, check food availability, and purchase meals, making campus dining inefficient and time-consuming, particularly during peak hours.

## Proposed Solution

We propose a Unified Campus Dining Management System for Krea University that brings campus cafeterias and the dining hall onto a single platform, enabling users to order food from cafeterias, access dining hall menus and nutritional information, view real-time food availability, and track their orders from one application.

## Validation

The proposed system will be evaluated by measuring reductions in waiting time and unnecessary movement across campus, improvements in order management and user satisfaction, and increased accessibility to campus dining information.

## Summary

University campuses rely on multiple dining facilities, including cafeterias, food outlets, and dining halls, to meet the daily food requirements of students, faculty, staff, and visitors. At Krea University, these facilities cater to a diverse community with different schedules, dietary preferences, and meal requirements. Since dining is an essential part of campus life, students need quick and reliable access to information about available food options and services to make informed dining decisions throughout the day.

Food ordering applications have simplified the process of browsing menus, placing orders, and making payments for individual eateries, while university dining halls typically publish daily meal schedules and nutritional information through separate channels. These systems effectively support their respective services but are designed to function independently. As a result, users interact with multiple platforms or sources of information depending on whether they wish to order food from a cafeteria or check the dining hall menu.

Because campus dining services are fragmented across different systems, users often have to switch between multiple sources of information and physically visit eateries to determine food availability before making a decision. During peak hours, this contributes to long queues, unnecessary movement across campus, and delays in receiving food. In addition, the lack of a single point of access makes it difficult for users to compare available dining options, plan meals according to their dietary preferences or nutritional requirements, and efficiently access campus dining services.

To address these challenges, this project proposes a Unified Campus Dining Management System for Krea University that integrates campus cafeterias and the dining hall into a single digital platform. The system enables users to browse cafeteria menus, place and track food orders, view real-time food availability, and access dining hall menus along with nutritional information from one application. Authorised staff can update menu availability and inventory in real time, while additional features such as estimated waiting times, dietary filters, notifications, customer feedback, and an interactive campus dining map further improve the overall dining experience and support informed decision-making.

The effectiveness of the proposed system will be evaluated using both operational and user-centred metrics. Performance measures will include reductions in average waiting time, unnecessary movement across campus, order inaccuracies, and food wastage, together with improvements in order management, inventory visibility, and operational efficiency. User satisfaction surveys, adoption rates, and usability assessments will be used to evaluate the platform's accessibility, convenience, and overall usefulness. By integrating all campus dining services into a single platform, the proposed system aims to provide a more efficient, convenient, and informed dining experience for the Krea University community.
